README — Archive of Nika Sandler
(Prepared for long-term preservation)

If you are reading this text, some time has passed.
This archive was created to outlive platforms, technologies,
political regimes, and the life of its author.

────────────────────────────────

AUTHOR

Nika Sandler
Contemporary artist working at the intersection of photography,
post-photography and artificial intelligence.

This archive contains artistic works and texts produced in the early
21st century.

────────────────────────────────

TIME AND CONTEXT

The works in this archive were created during a period marked by:

– rapid expansion of artificial intelligence,
– digital surveillance and censorship in many parts of the world,
– ecological anxiety and planetary instability,
– a growing interest in archives, non-human agency,
  and alternative forms of memory.

Many projects reflect these conditions directly or indirectly.

PRESERVATION AND INTEGRITY

The archive follows a distributed preservation approach. 
Its long-term accessibility does not depend on a single repository, 
storage provider, or physical medium.

Digital preservation measures include:

– the use of open and widely documented file formats whenever possible;
– the maintenance of multiple archival copies across different storage systems;
– periodic integrity checks of files stored in distributed repositories;
– the use of SHA-256 checksums to detect unintended changes to file contents;
– the use of PAR2 recovery data for selected archival packages;
– periodic checks of file accessibility, readability, and recoverability.

Checksums provide a means of detecting changes to files. 
Recovery data may help reconstruct damaged 
or missing portions of selected archival packages.

These mechanisms serve different purposes and complement one another.

The presence of a checksum does not, by itself, guarantee that a file remains readable, 
and the existence of a recovery package does not guarantee recovery in every situation.

Preservation copies are maintained independently of the availability 
of any single online platform whenever possible.

The archive is subject to ongoing maintenance. 
Preservation practices may evolve as storage technologies, 
file formats, and available resources change.

Any necessary migration, repair, or modification should preserve 
the original materials whenever possible and be documented transparently.

No digital preservation strategy can guarantee indefinite survival. 
The purpose of these measures is to reduce the risk of loss 
and increase the possibility of future access and recovery.

────────────────────────────────

WEB PRESERVATION

The archive includes preserved representations of websites, online publications, 
and other web-based materials associated with the artist's practice.

These materials document not only individual artworks 
but also their historical presentation, 
publication context, and presence within the digital environment 
in which they originally circulated.

Web preservation materials may include:

– archived versions of the artist's websites;
– captures of project pages and online portfolios;
– archived publications and exhibition-related materials;
– preserved web pages associated with individual projects;
– supporting texts, images, and publication metadata.

Web captures are maintained through available web archiving systems 
and independent archival copies.

The preservation of web materials is intended to retain contextual information 
that may not be present in the original artwork files alone.

Archived web pages may not reproduce every element of their original appearance or functionality. 
External services, embedded media, scripts, and linked resources may become unavailable over time.

The preservation of a web capture should therefore be understood 
as a record of a particular digital presentation, 
rather than a guarantee of complete reconstruction of the original website.

────────────────────────────────

PRESERVATION MAINTENANCE AND RECORDS

The archive is maintained through periodic checks 
of its distributed copies and storage systems.

Maintenance activities may include:

– verifying file integrity against available SHA-256 checksums;
– checking the accessibility of online archival copies;
– downloading and testing selected files and archival packages;
– verifying the integrity of compressed packages and associated PAR2 recovery data;
– checking whether files remain readable with available software;
– reviewing the condition and accessibility of physical storage media;
– updating repository documentation and archival references.

Preservation checks are intended to identify potential problems before 
they result in permanent data loss.

Where possible, significant maintenance activities, integrity checks, migrations, 
and recovery operations are recorded separately from the main README.

The maintenance record complements the archival metadata 
and provides a history of preservation activities.

The frequency and scope of maintenance may vary depending on the storage system, 
available resources, and the condition of individual archival copies.

PROJECTS INCLUDED

The World of Hedonia (2021)

On New Year's Eve, I made a wish to immerse myself forever
in the sensations that I experienced while taking tranquilizers.
I wanted to fall into a sweet sleep and not face reality anymore.
In an effort to forget myself, I was trapped in a depressive
hedonia, which Mark Fisher described in Capitalist Realism
as an inability to do anything other than the search for pleasure.

In this series, I depict different types of pleasures and the
states associated with them. I create still lifes and environments
from objects and substances that are associated with something
pleasant and seductive, and sometimes creepy. The doll appears
to be my alter-ego, living a New Year's experiment.

---

My Nonhuman Friends (2021)

"...the little turtle gazed longingly with her black, tender eyes
at the lying man. What was in her mind now? Perhaps a magical
thought of curiosity about a mysterious giant man, perhaps
the sadness of a dormant mind."

Andrey Platonov, "Soul".

Since birth, I have always been surrounded by cats. The first
being who became interested in me after I was discharged from
the hospital was a cat. He stood on the bed and stared at me
like a Platonov's turtle. He had never seen a human child before.
Fortunately, he liked me. I had been under his watchful eye ever
since. At the same time, my friendship with cats began.

A year ago, I lost a close feline friend. This event made me
immerse myself in the memories of cats and reflect on their
impact on me and my life.

In this series, I turn to archival photographs of my nonhuman
friends, some of which I magnify greatly and try to look at
and recall the smallest details in a catlike, far-sighted way.
I create still lifes with objects that remember the touches
and smell of my friends. I also take screenshots of camera
footage attached to cats' collars to get an idea of how they
saw the world.

---

Mycophilia (2022)

In "The Romance of the Fungus World", researchers Rolfe write
that in literature, folklore and science, fungus are often
associated with pestilence, death and decay, and their
physicality appears creepy and repulsive.

In this series, I move away from the mycophobic images of
fungoid entities in terms of conventional ideas and show
their ability to be creative and hospitable. I depict fungus
as being like unearthly creatures, as in myths and theories
about their cosmic origin.

---

Plant and Human Pain (2022)

I think, like modern biologists, that plants feel pain. They
found out that plants secrete substances that suppress it and
react to anesthetics like animals and people.

In my works, I show plants unconventionally beautiful: painful,
dying, dead. I compare the external manifestations of their
illnesses and deaths with the bodily suffering and dying of
people. Thus, I try to find common ground between the tragic
side of plant and human existence.

---

The Black Sun of Autocracy (2022)

In "The Black Sun of Autocracy", I travel to autocratic
countries* using Google Street View. I find and capture
digital artifacts that remind me of the essence of this regime.
The protagonist of this series is the black circle, aka the
black sun that shone over these countries.

*Autocracy (from the Greek. αὐτοϰρᾴτεια) is a form of
government that represents the unlimited sovereignty of
one person in the state.

---

A History of Teeth (2023)

In this work I explore teeth as a record of human experience.
I delve into the sensual and emotional world they reveal.
I also try to look beyond human perception by studying plaque
bacteria and machine vision.

---

Commission for Sensored (2023)

I seek eroticism in inanimate nature. I gaze in love with those
who have found eternity and interact sensually with them.
Through this, I strive to know assenting to life in death.

---

The Ancient Depths (2024)

In light of the increasingly concerning news about the imminent
fate of the oceans, I find myself contemplating the issue of
extinction with greater frequency. In collaboration with
artificial intelligence, I dive into ancient waters and capture
the inhabitants whose existence was interrupted and whose
evidence is preserved in fossils. The aim of this work is to
illustrate the fragility of life and to prompt reflection on
the current, rapid extinction in which humanity plays a
pivotal role.

ARCHIVAL CONTINUITY

This archive is designed to remain accessible independently of the continued participation of its original creator.

Its preservation strategy does not rely on a single individual, institution, repository, or online service.

If one repository, platform, or storage system becomes unavailable, other documented copies and archival references 
may provide alternative access routes.

Future custodians, researchers, or institutions encountering this archive are encouraged to:

– preserve existing archival copies before undertaking modifications;
– verify available checksums whenever possible;
– retain associated metadata, documentation, and recovery files;
– maintain relationships between original works and their derivative versions;
– document any migration, repair, or changes to archival packages;
– preserve the original file structures and filenames whenever possible;
– update archival references when new preservation locations become available.

If a particular storage system becomes inaccessible, surviving copies may be used to restore the affected materials, 
provided that their integrity can be established.

The archival portal and this README provide starting points 
for understanding the archive's structure and locating its distributed components.

No individual repository should be assumed to contain the complete archive.

The long-term continuity of this archive depends on the preservation of its materials, 
documentation, relationships, and contextual information.
